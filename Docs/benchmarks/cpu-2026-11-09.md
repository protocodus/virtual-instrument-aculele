# CPU at 0.4.57 and 0.4.58, 2026-11-09

0.4.57 puts the strings' 1e-30 floor on the states of the longitudinal ring
(`renderStringAxialForce`). It renders the same samples as 0.4.56
(`4d4687a`). A 4-core Intel Xeon VM at 2.10 GHz, GCC 13.3 `-O3`, 48 kHz,
256-sample blocks, single-threaded, the host's denormal mode left as it
starts (no flush to zero). Development-machine figures, not Reason's CPU
meter.

## Method

The portfolio's CPU ceiling (U12, 2026-11-09) judges each 256-sample block
on its fastest of three repeats. The shared renderer does not time blocks
that way yet, so an off-tree harness did: it plays each preset demo from
`Docs/audio/midi/` through the Performer as `RenderAculelePresetDemos`
does (each message at its own sample), three times, keeps each block's
fastest time, and reports the mean, the 99th percentile and the worst block
as a share of the 5.33 ms period. It also plays a stress phrase through
every preset: the sustain pedal down, an eight-note C major chord (C4-E6) at
velocity 127 at 0.05 s, its notes re-struck one by one every 0.25 s from
1 s, an eight-note D minor chord (D4-F6) on top at 3.5 s, all released at
6 s, the pedal up at 6.5 s, 9.5 s rendered. The shared renderer has no
stress phrase yet; this is one of the weight U12 describes. Baseline and
candidate ran alternately, twice each; the ranges below are both runs.

## Where the time went

Timing the tails with the processor's flush-to-zero turned on inside one
function at a time put the slow blocks in one place: the longitudinal ring
of each string. A string whose stretch sum the quiet-string gate has stopped,
or whose note is held under the pedal, keeps ringing its longitudinal wave
for as long as the voice is played; the ring's Thiran and loss states and
its output band's biquad states decay through the subnormal range and stay
there. Flushing them inside that function alone took the stress phrase's
mean from 13.1% to 8.0% of the period, the same as flushing the whole
engine (7.8%). Elsewhere a whole-engine flush moves the means by 0.3% or
less, within the run-to-run spread.

## Result

Mean and worst block, as a share of the 256-sample period.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Demos (7) | 7.2-10.6% | 7.2-9.4% | 19.1-27.8% | 18.8-26.2% |
| Tenor Fluorocarbon Fingerstyle's tail | | | 22.8-23.1% | 15.8-15.9% |
| Stress phrase (7 presets) | 11.0-13.5% | 7.3-8.4% | 27.3-31.9% after its first chord | 19.1-21.5% |
| Stress phrase, first chord | | | 54.8-96.6% | 55.4-98.5% |

- **Sound.** Against 0.4.56, the seven demos and the stress phrase through
  all seven presets render bit for bit (stereo float output, every sample).
  `Aculele.AudioDemos` and `Aculele.Levels` pass.
- **Why the profiles missed it.** Callgrind counts instructions, and a
  subnormal operand costs cycles, not instructions, so the 2026-11-07
  profile could not show it.
- **Reason.** Whether Reason runs a Rack Extension with flush-to-zero set is
  not measured here. Where it does, this change saves nothing there; on an
  x86 host that leaves subnormals on, it saves what it saves here.

## What remained over the ceiling at 0.4.57

Every render was still above U12's 10% for its worst block. Two things made
those blocks, neither changed in 0.4.57:

- **The dispersion designs made ahead.** For the first seconds after a
  preset loads, each block carries one step of a design's search
  (`prepareDispersion`). Measured inside the engine, one preset's whole
  warm-up is 19-108 ms of CPU in 103-335 steps, the largest step 0.4-1.1 ms
  (8-21% of a period). Most demo worst blocks (1.0-1.6 s) are these steps
  on top of a ringing chord.
- **A chord before the designs are ready.** The stress phrase's first chord
  arrives at 0.05 s, so each of its notes runs its own search at the note
  (README, Known gaps). That block takes 55-98% of the period. The same
  chord played 10 s after the preset loads takes 17.5%.

## 0.4.58: every design made in prepare, silent until ready

The owner's answer (2026-11-09): the instrument may stay silent until it is
ready. 0.4.58 makes every slot's design in `prepare()`, so a prepared engine
is ready (`AculeleEngine::ready`) and its first notes find their designs.
While it is not ready, after a body, strings, tuning, string age or rate
change, the Performer ignores note-ons; the slots are made again one step
every 5.3 ms as before, and an unready engine checks up to sixteen made
slots a step, so a change that leaves the designs alone (Wood) is ready
again within ten steps. A reset that adopts no new construction stays
ready.

One run of the same harness:

| Render | mean | p99 | worst |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Demos (7) | 6.9-9.0% | 10.5-15.2% | 15.2-19.0% |
| Stress phrase (7 presets) | 6.7-7.4% | 10.0-11.5% | 16.3-17.4% |

- **Sound.** The seven demos and the stress phrase through all seven
  presets render bit for bit as 0.4.57.
- **Blocks.** The worst block of any render falls from 98% to 19%. The
  stress phrase's worst is now its second chord's eight note-ons at 3.5 s.
- **Prepare.** 79-153 ms per factory preset at 48 kHz, from 75-82 ms;
  81-253 ms at 44.1 kHz, where nail strokes add the 48 kHz reference;
  66-241 ms at 96 and 192 kHz. Reason's adapter prepares one performer per
  oversampling factor when a device is created.
- **Unready time.** After a Shape change, 0.6-2.4 s on the factory presets
  at 48 kHz; after a Wood change, 2,560 samples (`Aculele.Ready`).
- **In Reason.** The adapter prepares on the default patch, so a song whose
  patch has another body, strings, tuning or age is silent for about that
  long after it loads. Not measured in Reason.

Every render is still above U12's 10% for its worst block.

The steady cost that is left is the work the 2026-11-07 profile names: the
stretch sum (32% of Concert Strum's instructions), the body modes (17%) and
the bridge modes (9%), all already four wide.
