# CPU on 2026-11-16: unheard body groups, four-lane sums and a kept cell layout

Engine 0.4.68 (`a0fd7cd`) against 0.4.69. The order of work was: exact changes
first, then changes that move only the last bits, and only after those any
candidate an ear might catch, which the owner gets as a listening pair. CPU
falls 11.3% across the seven demos and stress phrases on the build nearest the
one Reason makes, 10.4% at `-O3` and 8.2% on the native build. No render moves
by more than 122 dB under its own RMS.

## What changed

1. **Unheard body groups are left at rest** (`BodyBank::load`, `render`). At
   44.1-192 kHz, slots 8-15 and 140-159 of the body bank have residues in no
   Shape and Wood. That is seven groups of four modes. Such a group can never
   reach the output, so `render` neither steps it nor sums it, and `load`
   keeps its states at zero (`bodyGroupsHeard_`, built once per rate in
   `prepareBodyConfigurations`). A group that has no residues in the current
   bank but does in another Shape or Wood is still stepped, so a later change
   to that bank finds it ringing (`sameBodyBank`). **Exact.**
2. **The body's contributions gather in four lanes** and are added across the
   lanes once per sample, where each mode used to join one running sum in
   turn. Each mode's states are unchanged bit for bit. Only the order of the
   additions into the output moves. **Last bits:** the worst of the 63 renders
   is 131.9 dB under its RMS, with a peak of -125.4 dBFS.
3. **The body flushes its tiny states every 16 samples** where it used to
   flush every sample. In 16 samples the fastest-decaying mode (at 8 kHz)
   falls 76 dB. A state just over the 1e-30 floor would need 178 dB more to
   reach a subnormal, and a state under 1e-30 adds less than a float's last
   bit to any sum it could be heard in. **Identical on all 63 renders**, with
   and without change 2.
4. **The strings' stretch sum gathers in four lanes under the SDK and on
   ARM64** (`sumAxialCells`), as it already did on native x86-64. Until now
   the SDK and ARM64 paths had kept the scalar order. Native x86-64 is
   unchanged. **Last bits, carried further:** this sum feeds the strings, and
   the worst render is 122.1 dB under its RMS, with a peak of -118.5 dBFS.
   No ARM64 machine was available for this pass. There the change is the same
   reordering, but its renders were not measured.
5. **Each string loop keeps its cells' layout** (`StringAxialForce::Cells`).
   The layout holds the shear, the steps, the widths, the cell count and the
   two scales, which cost five divisions per loop per sample. It is made again
   only when the loop's period, the round trip, the stride or the axial-waves
   setting changes. Its key is the exact bits of those inputs, so a copied,
   cleared or restored voice can only miss the cache, never read a stale
   layout. **Exact.**

## The null tests

Each build renders 63 phrases as raw float, with stereo and piezo outputs:
- all seven presets;
- each preset's demo at 48 kHz in 256-sample blocks, at 44.1 kHz in 64-sample
  blocks and at 96 kHz in 512-sample blocks;
- the eight-note re-pluck stress phrase;
- a Shape change under ringing chords;
- pitch bends with a moving bridge hand;
- chords let go and then bent;
- a String Age sweep;
- a 12.5 s sweep of every control under re-struck chords with keyswitches,
  mod wheel and pressure.

For each render the residual is the RMS of the difference, in dB under that
render's RMS, and its peak in dBFS.

| Change | Reason-like build | Native build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1, unheard groups | identical, 63 of 63 | identical, 63 of 63 |
| 3, flush every 16 samples | identical, 63 of 63 | identical, 63 of 63 |
| 2, body sum in four lanes | -131.9 dB, peak -125.4 dBFS | -132.1 dB, peak -126.4 dBFS |
| 4, stretch sum in four lanes | -122.1 dB, peak -118.5 dBFS | no change |
| 5, kept cell layout | identical, 63 of 63 | identical, 63 of 63 |
| 0.4.69 against 0.4.68 | -122.1 dB, peak -118.5 dBFS | -132.1 dB, peak -126.4 dBFS |

As a scale for those figures, moving Pluck Position by one float step
(`nextafter`) changes 24 of the 63 renders, by up to 85.5 dB under their RMS.
So the engine carries a last-bit difference in a control through its strings,
bridge and body as far as that, without chaos. A reordered sum lands 37-47 dB
under that one-step control change.

## Timing

Development machine: a 4-vCPU Intel Xeon cloud VM on Linux. Compilers: Clang
18.1.3 and GCC 13.3. Three builds of the engine, the Performer and the same
harness:

- **Reason-like `-O2`:** Clang `-std=c++17 -O2 -fno-builtin -msse3
  -DJUKEBOX_SDK -DACULELE_EXTENDED_SAMPLE_RATES=1 -fnew-alignment=8`. This is
  Reason's compiler tier, flags and device layout, against glibc rather than
  Reason's maths library.
- **Reason-like `-O3`:** the same at `-O3`, as in the 2026-11-14 report.
- **Native:** GCC `-std=c++20 -O3`.

The harness measures the calling thread's CPU time over block processing
only, at 48 kHz in 256-sample blocks. Each preset plays its demo and then the
stress phrase. There are 8 rounds, with the order alternating 0.4.68 then
0.4.69 and 0.4.69 then 0.4.68, and each cell below is the median per phrase.

The `-O3` totals are 7162.4 ms and 6418.0 ms, and the native totals 7366.9 ms
and 6765.9 ms. Each step was also timed in its own paired run against the
step before it, on the Reason-like `-O3` build. Changes 1-3 together took
9.3%, and change 1 alone measured about neutral in time. Change 4 took a
further 2.0% and change 5 a further 1.3%. Change 4 does nothing on the native build, which is why that build
gains less.

The worst 256-sample block is each block's fastest of five runs, measured
twice per build on the Reason-like `-O2` build:
- Concert Strum's demo: 11.3-11.4% of the period to 10.5-11.2%.
- Low G Tenor's demo: 11.4-11.6% to 10.5-10.8%.
- Concert Piezo Stage's demo: 10.5-11.1% to 9.9-10.0%.
- The stress phrases: 9.3-10.4% to 8.6-9.4%.

The worst blocks are note starts over ringing strings, which these changes
touch least. These are development-machine figures, not Reason's CPU meter.

## Measured and not taken

| Candidate | CPU | Largest null | Why not |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The bridge's residue sums in three chains, not one | -0.8% | identical on 63 of 63 | The sums are double and their result is rounded to float, so the order hides, but that is not exact by construction. The comment beside the loop promises serial sums. |
| The bridge's modes in float, as first tried | +18.7% (slower) | -77.1 dB, peak -69.3 dBFS, at 96 kHz | Converting each state every sample cost more than it saved. A float bridge with float storage would trade the double precision the poles near the unit circle need at 192 kHz for a few percent at most. |
| The stretch evaluated every other sample | not timed | -17.9 dB | Audible, and against the stated reason for full-rate evaluation (aliased sum tones). |
| The quiet-string stretch gate at 30 dB, not 40 dB | -0.9% (-O2, on 0.4.69) | -59.0 dB, peak -41.4 dBFS, on the hand-then-bend stress phrase; every demo -80.7 dB or less | Barely audible at most. Offered to the owner as an A/B pair (`listening/2026-11-16-bent-ring` in the project files, key unread by design). Kept at 40 dB unless the owner picks the other letter. |

Two further ideas were left at the probe stage:
- Leaving out the bank's weakest modes or the body's moment drive. The
  per-mode energies did not single out modes that could go.
- Letting the piezo chain rest while it is not heard. That would differ when
  Capture or Piezo Mix changes under a ringing chord.

Neither was taken to a null test.
