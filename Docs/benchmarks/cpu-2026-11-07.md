# CPU at 0.4.46, 2026-11-07

A profile of the engine as it ships, to see whether anything is left for an
optimisation that renders the same samples. Nothing was changed. A 4-core
Intel Xeon VM at 2.10 GHz, GCC 13.3 `-O3`, 48 kHz, 256-sample blocks,
Concert Strum (preset 0), `AculeleProbe --float`.

## Where the time goes

Instructions under Callgrind, inclusive, as a share of the whole render.
The 6 s render is the preset's own demo; the 30 s render is a strummed
progression of ten chord shapes (frets 0-7) repeated for 26 s, so the
designs made ahead of the notes (`prepareDispersion`) are a warm-up and
not a steady cost.

| Function | 6 s | 30 s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `BodyBank::render` (232 body modes) | 19% | 21.4% |
| `renderStringAxialForce` | 18% | 20.1% |
| `BridgeLoad::advanceModes` | 10% | 11.6% |
| `prepareDispersion` (designs made ahead, `calibrateDispersion`) | 8.3% | 2.2% |
| `RoomAmbience::process` | 10% | 4.8% |
| `StringLoop::readDelay` | - | 3.2% |
| `configureVoice` (note-ons) | - | 2.9% |

The three per-sample loops at the top are already vectorised four wide
(the body modes as complex rotations with a bit-mask denormal flush, the
stretch cells as plain offset reads, 2026-11-05). The 8% the dispersion
designs take in a short profile is the 76 designs (4 strings x 19 frets)
made in the first seconds after a preset loads; over 30 s they are 2%, and
in a session they end once every slot is ready. The 2.47 M `atan2` calls
in the 6 s profile are theirs, not the axial force's.

## Block cost

Wall time of each 256-sample block (5.33 ms of audio), the 30 s render,
three runs, blocks after the finger-lookahead skip. "Events" are blocks a
MIDI event lands in.

| Blocks | n | median | p99 | worst |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| first 4 s, no events | 690 | 0.36-0.39 ms | 0.85-1.03 ms | 0.88-3.14 ms |
| first 4 s, events | 59 | 0.42-0.46 ms | 0.75-1.35 ms | 0.75-1.35 ms |
| after 4 s, no events | 4535 | 0.35-0.36 ms | 0.51-0.62 ms | 1.42-2.10 ms |
| after 4 s, events | 341 | 0.41-0.42 ms | 0.63-0.69 ms | 0.68-0.95 ms |

Mean 0.37 ms a block, 7.0% of the period. A block with a note-on costs a
median 0.06 ms more than one without. The designs made ahead raise the
first seconds' 99th percentile by about 0.5 ms. The worst blocks
(1.4-3.1 ms, 27-59% of the period) fall in blocks with no events, in
different places on each run, so they are the VM's scheduling, not the
engine's work. Development-machine figures, not Reason's CPU meter.

## Conclusion

No change is made. The remaining time is in loops that are already
vectorised with bit-exact care; the only large single item in a short
profile is warm-up work that is already spread one search start at a
time. An optimisation from here would have to change the arithmetic, and
so the samples.
