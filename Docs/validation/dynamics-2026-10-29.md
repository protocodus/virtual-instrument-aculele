# Dynamics check, 29 October 2026

The owner asked for natural dynamics. This note records how Aculele 0.4.19's
level and colour follow MIDI velocity. Nothing in it changed the engine.

## How it was measured

- Single E4 notes (MIDI 64) on Concert Strum, each a fresh engine, so every
  note takes the same random draws. They were rendered with `AculeleProbe`
  at 48 kHz in 256-sample blocks, with `--picking` 0-3 for Finger, Pick, Thumb
  and Strum.
- Level is the RMS of the first second, read against velocity 127. Colour is
  each band's share of the power from 5 to 155 ms, and the partials H2-H12
  against H1 from 20 to 170 ms.
- A second ladder played the same velocities one after another on one engine,
  2 s apart and held for 1.8 s, as a player would.

## Level

The owner's law is linear in force, so velocity 64 should read
20 log10(64/127) = -5.95 dB against velocity 127. From velocity 40 up,
Finger lands within 0.15 dB of the law at every step of 8. The other
techniques, measured at 64 and 96, land within 0.25 dB. Below velocity 40,
a nail's or plectrum's slip makes soft notes quieter:

| Velocity | Law | Finger | Pick | Thumb | Strum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | -24.0 | -29.5 | | | |
| 16 | -18.0 | -20.1 | -19.9 | -18.3 | -20.4 |
| 32 | -12.0 | -12.4 | -12.5 | -12.3 | -12.5 |
| 64 | -5.95 | -5.85 | -6.0 | -6.2 | -5.9 |
| 96 | -2.43 | -2.36 | -2.46 | -2.51 | -2.40 |

Velocity 8 was rendered for Finger only. The soft end is the 0.4.18
behaviour, which was adopted without a listening verdict: an edge that keeps
its size still lets go more slowly when the string moves slowly
(`Docs/decisions.md`, 2026-10-28).

When the notes follow one another, they scatter by up to about 2 dB around
the law. The ladder's step from velocity 48 to 56 reads +0.1 dB, and the step
from 88 to 96 reads -0.3 dB. The scatter comes from the hand's ±1 dB force
residual and from each pluck's own contact point, both chosen by ear on
2026-10-28. Fresh notes show neither, so they are what show the law.

## Colour

Finger, Pick and Strum all brighten steadily with force. For Finger, the
2.5-6 kHz band's share rises from -35.2 dB at velocity 8 to -24.7 dB at 127,
and the 6-16 kHz band's from -63.3 to -54.5 dB. No step between neighbouring
velocities is larger than 2.1 dB.

Thumb is uneven. As velocity rises, H2 falls from -6.5 to -16.2 dB against H1.
H3 falls to -18.8 dB at velocity 64, then climbs back to -10.1 dB at 127.
Sweeping Touch at velocity 64 gives the same pattern, and it is still there
0.5-0.9 s after the pluck. So it lies in the released wave, not in the attack
noise.

Touch moves three things: the contact's width, the slip time of the release,
and the release angle. The width and the slip each scale every partial's
magnitude monotonically. The slip also turns each partial's phase, and the
coupled bridge answers to that phase. This was not traced any further.
Nothing measured describes how a real thumb's colour changes with force, so
no change is grounded.

## Result

The dynamics follow the owner's force law and brighten with force on three
of the four techniques. Nothing here licenses an engine change.
